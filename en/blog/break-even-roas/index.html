Is your ad actually profitable? A guide to break-even ROAS

November 10, 2026 · 2 min read

The question I hear most from business owners: "My ROAS is 2x, is that good?" The honest answer: it depends on your profit margin. The same number can be great for one business and a loss for another.

What is ROAS?

ROAS is return on ad spend: the revenue your ads generated divided by what you spent on them.

  • You spent $500 and ads brought $1,500 in sales → ROAS = 3x

The catch is that revenue isn't profit. From that $1,500 you still pay for the product, shipping, packaging and more.

The break-even formula

Break-even ROAS is the point where your ads neither make nor lose money:

Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ profit margin

Quick examples:

  • 50% margin → break-even at 2x
  • 40% margin → break-even at 2.5x
  • 25% margin → break-even at 4x

So if your margin is 25% and your ROAS is 3x, you're losing money even though the number looks good.

How much can you pay per order?

The same idea applies to cost per acquisition (CPA). The highest CPA you can afford without a loss:

Max CPA = average order value × profit margin

With a $40 average order and a 40% margin, the most you can pay per order is $16. Anything below that is profit.

3 common mistakes

  1. Calculating margin without shipping and returns. If 10% of orders come back, your real margin is lower than you think.
  2. Judging a campaign after 3 days. The algorithm needs time to learn, especially for sales campaigns.
  3. Ignoring customer lifetime value. If customers buy two or three times a year, you can afford a higher CPA on the first order.
