Campaign Analysis

How to read your campaign results: metrics that matter more than views

November 17, 20262 min read

You open Ads Manager and see dozens of columns. The problem isn't a lack of data. It's knowing which number to look at first. The best approach is to read the campaign as a funnel: from the moment the ad appears to the moment of sale.

1. Does the ad stop people?

Before anything else, do people stop on your ad? For video ads, compare how many watched the first few seconds with the number of impressions. If it's low, the problem is the opening of the video, not the targeting or the price.

2. Do they click?

Link click-through rate (link CTR) tells you whether the message and the offer are convincing enough for someone to click. A weak CTR with good viewing means the video attracts attention but the offer doesn't convince.

3. Do they complete the step?

Conversion rate on the landing page, form or chat. If people click but don't complete, review page speed, offer clarity and the number of steps required.

4. What does the result cost?

Cost per lead or cost per acquisition (CPL / CPA), then return on ad spend (ROAS). These are the decision numbers: increase the budget, adjust the campaign, or stop it? Always compare them with your own break-even point, not with other businesses' numbers.

5. Is the audience getting tired?

Frequency tells you how many times each person has seen the same ad. When frequency rises and costs start climbing, the ad usually needs a new idea or design.

An important rule: don't judge too fast

Editing a campaign every day sends it back into the learning phase. Meta's long-standing guidance is that an ad set generally needs around 50 optimization events within 7 days to exit that phase, though the threshold can vary by campaign type. Give the campaign enough time and data before judging it, unless the numbers are far from acceptable.

A practical shortcut

  • Weak viewing: change the opening of the ad.
  • Good viewing, weak clicks: change the offer or the message.
  • Good clicks, weak conversion: review the page, the form or your reply speed.
  • Good conversion, high cost: review the audience, the budget and the competition.
