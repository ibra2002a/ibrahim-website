Many ads are judged by views and likes. Those numbers look nice, but they don't pay salaries. **Performance marketing** starts from a different question: what business result do we want, and how much can we afford to pay for it?

## The idea in simple terms

With traditional advertising you pay for exposure and hope it turns into sales. With performance marketing you define a measurable goal from day one, such as a purchase, a lead or a booking, then build the campaign, the tracking and the reporting around it.

## The metrics that actually matter

- **Cost per lead (CPL):** what each person who leaves their details or messages you costs.
- **Cost per acquisition (CPA):** what each customer who actually buys costs.
- **Return on ad spend (ROAS):** how much comes back for every unit you spend on ads.
- **Conversion rate:** the share of visitors who complete the step you want.

Views and engagement are still useful for understanding the ad itself, but they are not the final verdict.

## Three conditions for it to work

1. **Correct tracking:** without the Pixel, Conversions API and proper UTM links, you're deciding on incomplete numbers.
2. **The right campaign objective:** pick engagement and the platform will bring you people who engage, not people who buy.
3. **Continuous testing:** test several ad ideas and audiences, then give the budget to whatever proves itself in the numbers.

## What this means for a business owner

It means asking about the result before the channel. Instead of "we want an Instagram ad", ask: "we want leads for our service, and the most we can pay per lead is X". The whole plan is built from there, and every decision becomes something you can review.
